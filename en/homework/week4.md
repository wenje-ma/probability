> **Definition 1.3.3** (Simple Function)<br>A function $\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ is called a **simple function** if $\phi\left(\omega\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{A_{m}}\left(\omega\right)$, where $c_{m}\in\mathbb R$, $A_{m}\in\mathcal F$.

> **Theorem 1.3.7** (Limit Operations on Random Variables)<br>If $X_{1},X_{2},\dots$ are random variables, then $\inf_{n}X_{n}$, $\sup_{n}X_{n}$, $\limsup_{n}X_{n}$, $\liminf_{n}X_{n}$ are all random variables. Hence $\left\{\omega:\lim_{n}X_{n}\text{ 存在}\right\}=\left\{\omega:\limsup_{n}X_{n}-\liminf_{n}X_{n}=0\right\}$ is a measurable set.

> **Formula 1.3.1** (The $\sigma$-Algebra Generated by $X$)<br>$\sigma\left(X\right)=\left\{\left\{X\in B\right\}:B\in\mathcal S\right\}$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra with respect to which $X$ is measurable.

### Problem 1

Let

$$
\phi\left(\omega\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{A_{m}}\left(\omega\right),
$$

where $c_{m}\in\mathbb R$, $A_{m}\in\mathcal F$. Prove that the class of $\mathcal F$-measurable functions is the smallest class containing all simple functions and closed under pointwise limits.

### Solution to Problem 1

Let $\mathcal M$ be the smallest class of functions containing all simple functions and closed under pointwise limits.

We first prove that every function in $\mathcal M$ is measurable. Simple functions are clearly measurable (**Definition 1.3.3**); the class of measurable functions is closed under pointwise limits (by **Theorem 1.3.7**, $\lim_{n}f_{n}=\limsup_{n}f_{n}$ is measurable), so by the minimality of $\mathcal M$, $\mathcal M\subset\left\{\text{可测函数}\right\}$.

Next, we prove that every measurable function $f$ belongs to $\mathcal M$. Decompose $f=f^{+}-f^{-}$, where $f^{+}=f\vee 0$ and $f^{-}=\left(-f\right)\vee 0$. For a nonnegative measurable function $g$, construct

$$
g_{n}\left(x\right)=\frac{\left[2^{n}g\left(x\right)\right]}{2^{n}}\wedge n,
$$

where $\left[x\right]$ is the greatest integer not exceeding $x$. $g_{n}$ is a step simple function taking finitely many values, and $g_{n}\uparrow g$ pointwise. Now take

$$
\begin{aligned}f_{n}&=f_{n}^{+}-f_{n}^{-},\\
f_{n}^{\pm}&=\frac{\left[2^{n}f^{\pm}\right]}{2^{n}}\wedge n.
\end{aligned}
$$

$f_{n}$ is the difference of two simple functions, hence still a simple function, so $f_{n}\in\mathcal M$; moreover $f_{n}\to f$ pointwise (since $f_{n}^{\pm}\to f^{\pm}$), and since $\mathcal M$ is closed under pointwise limits, $f\in\mathcal M$.

In summary, the class of measurable functions is exactly the smallest class containing simple functions and closed under pointwise limits. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 2

Using the conclusion of the previous problem, prove that $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma\left(X\right)$ if and only if $Y=f\left(X\right)$, where $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is measurable.

### Solution to Problem 2

$\left(\Leftarrow\right)$ If $Y=f\left(X\right)$ and $f$ is measurable, then for any Borel set $B$,

$$
\left\{Y\in B\right\}=\left\{X\in f^{-1}\left(B\right)\right\}\in\sigma\left(X\right)
$$

(since $f^{-1}\left(B\right)\in\mathcal R$, see **Formula 1.3.1**), so $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma\left(X\right)$.

$\left(\Rightarrow\right)$ Suppose $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma\left(X\right)$. First handle simple functions: if $\phi=\sum_{m}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{B_{m}}$ is a $\sigma\left(X\right)$-measurable simple function, then $B_{m}\in\sigma\left(X\right)$, so $B_{m}=\left\{X\in C_{m}\right\}$ for some $C_{m}\in\mathcal R$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\phi\\
&=\sum_{m}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{C_{m}}\left(X\right)\\
&=g\left(X\right),\\
g&=\sum_{m}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{C_{m}}\ \text{可测}.
\end{aligned}
$$

By Problem 1, $Y$ is the pointwise limit of a sequence of $\sigma\left(X\right)$-measurable simple functions $\phi_{n}$, with each $\phi_{n}=g_{n}\left(X\right)$ and $g_{n}$ measurable. Let $f=\limsup_{n}g_{n}$; then $f$ is measurable (**Theorem 1.3.7**), and for every $\omega$,

$$
g_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\to Y\left(\omega\right),
$$

hence

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;f\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\\
&=\limsup_{n}g_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\\
&=Y\left(\omega\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

i.e. $Y=f\left(X\right)$. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 3

Give a constructive proof of Problem 2: from

$$
\left\{m2^{-n}\le Y< \left(m+1\right)2^{-n}\right\}=\left\{X\in B_{m,n}\right\}
$$

for some $B_{m,n}\in\mathcal R$, let $f_{n}\left(x\right)=m2^{-n}$ ($x\in B_{m,n}$). Prove that as $n\to\infty$, $f_{n}\left(x\right)\to f\left(x\right)$ and $Y=f\left(X\right)$.

### Solution to Problem 3

Since $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma\left(X\right)$, the set

$$
\left\{m2^{-n}\le Y< \left(m+1\right)2^{-n}\right\}\in\sigma\left(X\right),
$$

so there exists $B_{m,n}\in\mathcal R$ such that this set equals $\left\{X\in B_{m,n}\right\}$. For each $n$, let

$$
f_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m}m2^{-n}\mathbf{1}_{B_{m,n}}\left(x\right),
$$

$f_{n}$ is a step simple function on $\mathbb R$, hence measurable. By construction, whenever $m2^{-n}\le Y\left(\omega\right)< \left(m+1\right)2^{-n}$ we have $f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)=m2^{-n}$; thus $f_{n}\left(X\right)$ is exactly $Y$ rounded down to a multiple of $2^{-n}$:

$$
f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\le Y\left(\omega\right)< f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)+2^{-n}.
$$

Hence

$$
\left|f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)-Y\left(\omega\right)\right|< 2^{-n}\to 0,
$$

i.e. $f_{n}\left(X\right)\to Y$ pointwise.

Let $f=\lim_{n}f_{n}$ (if some points do not converge, take $\limsup$ so that it is defined everywhere; by **Theorem 1.3.7**, $f$ is measurable). Then for every $\omega$

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;f\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\\
&=\lim_{n}f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\\
&=Y\left(\omega\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

i.e. $Y=f\left(X\right)$. $\blacksquare$
