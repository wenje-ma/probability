> **Theorem 1.1.1 (Properties of a Probability Measure)**
> 
> Let $\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P\right)$ be a probability space. Then: (1) $P\left(\varnothing\right)=0$; (2) monotonicity: $A\subset{B}\Rightarrow{P}\left(A\right)\le{P}\left(B\right)$; (3) if $A_{n}\uparrow{A}$ then $P\left(A_{n}\right)\uparrow{P}\left(A\right)$ (continuity from below), and if $A_{n}\downarrow{A}$ then $P\left(A_{n}\right)\downarrow{P}\left(A\right)$ (continuity from above).

> **Theorem 1.1.1 (Properties of Probability Measures)**
> 
> Let $\mu$ be a probability measure. Then: (1) $\mu\left(\varnothing\right)=0$; (2) countable additivity: for pairwise disjoint $\left\{E_{n}\right\}$, $\mu\left(\bigcup_{n}E_{n}\right)=\sum_{n}\mu\left(E_{n}\right)$; (3) continuity from below: if $E_{n}\uparrow{E}$, then $\mu\left(E_{n}\right)\uparrow\mu\left(E\right)$.

> **Formula 1.3.1 (The $\sigma$-algebra Generated by $X$)**
> 
> $\sigma\left(X\right)=\left\{\left\{X\in{B}\right\}:B\in\mathcal{S}\right\}$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra making $X$ measurable.

> **Definition 1.3.3 (Simple Function)**
> 
> A function $\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ is called a simple function if $\phi\left(\omega\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}c_{m}\mathbf{1}_{A_{m}}\left(\omega\right)$, where $c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}$ and $A_{m}\in\mathcal{F}$.

> **Theorem 1.3.7 (Limit Operations on Random Variables)**
> 
> If $X_{1},X_{2},\dots$ are random variables, then $\inf_{n}X_{n}$, $\sup_{n}X_{n}$, $\limsup_{n}X_{n}$, and $\liminf_{n}X_{n}$ are all random variables. Hence $\left\{\omega:\lim_{n}X_{n}\text{~exists}\right\}=\left\{\omega:\limsup_{n}X_{n}-\liminf_{n}X_{n}=0\right\}$ is a measurable set.

> **Definition 1.4.1 (Four-Step Definition of the Integral)**
> 
> Let $\mu$ be a $\sigma$-finite measure on $\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\right)$. (1) For a simple function $\phi=\sum_{i}a_{i}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}$ ($A_{i}$ pairwise disjoint, $\mu\left(A_{i}\right)<\infty$), $\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{i}a_{i}\mu\left(A_{i}\right)$; (2) for a nonnegative bounded $f$ vanishing outside a finite-measure set, $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sup_{\phi\le{f}}\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu$; (3) for a nonnegative measurable $f$, $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sup\left\{\int{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu:0\le{h}\le{f},\,h\text{~bounded~and~}\mu\left(\left\{h>0\right\}\right)<\infty\right\}$; (4) for a general measurable $f$, if $\int\left|f\right|\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\infty$ then $f$ is integrable, and $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int{f}^{+}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int{f}^{-}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$, where $f^{+}=f\vee0$ and $f^{-}=\left(-f\right)\vee0$.

> **Theorem 1.4.7 (Basic Properties of the Integral)**
> 
> Let $f,g$ be integrable. Then: (1) if $f\ge0$ almost everywhere, then $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\ge0$; (2) for $a\in\mathbb{R}$, $\int{a}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=a\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$; (3) $\int\left(f+g\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int{g}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$; (4) if $g\le{f}$ almost everywhere, then $\int{g}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\le\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$.

> **Theorem 1.5.7 (Monotone Convergence Theorem)**
> 
> If $0\le{f}_{n}$ and $f_{n}\uparrow{f}$ pointwise, then $\int{f}_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\uparrow\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu$.

## 1

Give a constructive proof of Solution 2: from

$$
\left\{m2^{-n}\le{Y}<\left(m+1\right)2^{-n}\right\}=\left\{X\in{B}_{m,n}\right\}
$$

(for some $B_{m,n}\in\mathcal{R}$), set $f_{n}\left(x\right)=m2^{-n}$ for $x\in{B}_{m,n}$, and prove that $f_{n}\left(x\right)\to{f}\left(x\right)$ as $n\to\infty$ and that $Y=f\left(X\right)$.

### Solution to 1

Since $Y$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma\left(X\right)$, the set

$$
\left\{m2^{-n}\le{Y}<\left(m+1\right)2^{-n}\right\}\in\sigma\left(X\right),
$$

so there exists $B_{m,n}\in\mathcal{R}$ such that this set equals $\left\{X\in{B}_{m,n}\right\}$. For each $n$, set

$$
f_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m}m2^{-n}\mathbf{1}_{B_{m,n}}\left(x\right).
$$

Each $f_{n}$ is a step simple function on $\mathbb{R}$, hence measurable. By construction, whenever $m2^{-n}\le{Y}\left(\omega\right)<\left(m+1\right)2^{-n}$, one has $f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)=m2^{-n}$; thus $f_{n}\left(X\right)$ is exactly $Y$ rounded down to the nearest multiple of $2^{-n}$:

$$
f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)\le{Y}\left(\omega\right)<{}f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)+2^{-n}.
$$

Hence

$$
\left|f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)-Y\left(\omega\right)\right|<2^{-n}\to0,
$$

i.e. $f_{n}\left(X\right)\to{Y}$ pointwise.

Set $f=\lim_{n}f_{n}$ (if it fails to converge at some points, take $\limsup$ to define it everywhere; by **Theorem 1.3.7 (Limit Operations on Random Variables)**, $f$ is measurable). Then for every $\omega$,

$$
f\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)=\lim_{n}f_{n}\left(X\left(\omega\right)\right)=Y\left(\omega\right),
$$

i.e. $Y=f\left(X\right)$. $\blacksquare$

## 2

Prove: if $f\ge0$ and $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0$, then $f=0$ almost everywhere.

### Solution to 2

Let $E=\left\{x:f\left(x\right)>0\right\}$. For each $n\ge1$, set $E_{n}=\left\{x:f\left(x\right)\ge1/n\right\}$; then $E_{n}\uparrow{E}$, so

$$
\mu\left(E\right)=\lim_{n}\mu\left(E_{n}\right)
$$

(continuity from below of the measure, **Theorem 1.1.1 (Properties of a Probability Measure)**, condition (3)).

Suppose for contradiction that $\mu\left(E\right)>0$; then there exists $n$ such that $\mu\left(E_{n}\right)>0$. Since $f\ge\left(1/n\right)\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}$ and by monotonicity of the integral (**Theorem 1.4.7 (Basic Properties of the Integral)**, conditions (1) and (4)),

$$
\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\ge\int\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}_{E_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{1}{n}\mu\left(E_{n}\right)>0,
$$

contradicting $\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0$. Hence $\mu\left(E\right)=0$, i.e. $f=0$ almost everywhere. $\blacksquare$

## 3

Let $f\ge0$ and

$$
E_{n,m}=\left\{x:m/2^{n}\le{f}\left(x\right)<\left(m+1\right)/2^{n}\right\}.
$$

Prove that as $n\uparrow\infty$,

$$
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{2^{n}}\mu\left(E_{n,m}\right)\uparrow\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

### Solution to 3

For each $n$, define the simple function

$$
f_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{2^{n}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{n,m}}\left(x\right).
$$

By the definition of $E_{n,m}$, at points where $f$ takes finite values,

$$
f_{n}\left(x\right)\le{f}\left(x\right)<{}f_{n}\left(x\right)+2^{-n},
$$

so $f_{n}\uparrow{f}$ pointwise (dyadic subdivision makes $f_{n}$ nondecreasing and approximate $f$). By the definition of the integral of a simple function (**Definition 1.4.1 (Four-Step Definition of the Integral)**, condition (1)),

$$
\int{f}_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{2^{n}}\mu\left(E_{n,m}\right).
$$

By **Theorem 1.5.7 (Monotone Convergence Theorem)**, since $f_{n}\uparrow{f}$,

$$
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{2^{n}}\mu\left(E_{n,m}\right)=\int{f}_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\uparrow\int{f}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

This is exactly the desired conclusion. $\blacksquare$
